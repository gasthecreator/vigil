## 2. Scientific Objective
Evaluate whether Vigil's CP-SAT Radiotherapy Continuity Scheduler (`RadiotherapyScheduler.solve`) safely mitigates excess cancer recurrence harm and acute palliative complications during machine breakdowns, under severe equipment scarcity and high demand (utilisation $\ge 90\%$, waits extending into weeks), while strictly enforcing algorithmic fairness and contiguity.

---

## 3. Pre-Registered Hypotheses

### Hypothesis H1 — Harm Reduction vs First-Come-First-Served (FCFS)
* **Statement:** The true `Vigil M7 CP-SAT Solver` will achieve lower mean cumulative patient harm than FCFS across breakdown and scarcity scenarios.
* **Criterion:** Relative harm reduction $\ge 10.0\%$ with paired bootstrap 95% confidence interval strictly excluding zero ($\text{CI}_{\text{lower}} > 0$).

### Hypothesis H2 — Optimiser vs Clinical Urgency Heuristic
* **Statement:** Under realistic department operations, the CP-SAT solver will achieve comparable or superior harm reduction relative to the static `Clinical Urgency Heuristic` (Palliative > TNBC > PMRT > Lumpectomy), while guaranteeing operational constraints (no-starvation $W_{\max}$, course contiguity, machine switching stability, bounded disparity) that the unconstrained heuristic violates.
* **Reporting Protocol:** If the heuristic achieves lower raw realised harm under any regime, this result will be reported prominently, accompanied by an explicit investigation of the gap between the multi-objective optimisation objective and realised harm.

### Hypothesis H3 — Subgroup Fairness under Scarcity
* **Statement:** Under high scarcity ($\ge 90\%$ utilisation, wait times extending to weeks), the CP-SAT solver enforces the bounded wait disparity constraint across demographic subgroups ($\le 5.0$ days), whereas unconstrained greedy or urgency policies produce larger disparities.
* **Criterion:** Mean subgroup wait time disparity for CP-SAT $\le 5.0$ days.

### Hypothesis H4 — Ranking Stability under Parameter Uncertainty
* **Statement:** Triage priority orderings generated by the harm model remain stable when parameters are perturbed across their pre-registered assumption ranges.
* **Criterion:** Spearman rank correlation $\ge 0.80$ between baseline and perturbed parameter evaluations across diverse patient cohorts.

## 4. Policy Arms
1. **`Vigil M7 CP-SAT Solver`:** Calls `RadiotherapyScheduler.solve(machines, patients, horizon_days=14)` at every decision step in rolling horizon. Solves constrained integer program minimizing total expected piecewise harm, switching penalties, and interruption penalties subject to hard daily capacity, downtime, $W_{\max}$, and equity bounds.
2. **`Clinical Urgency Heuristic`:** Static clinical triage rule prioritizing Palliative > TNBC > PMRT > Lumpectomy, sub-sorted by arrival delay.
3. **`First-Come-First-Served (FCFS)`:** Pure queue ordering based strictly on arrival date / elapsed delay.
4. **`Greedy Marginal Harm (Proxy)`:** Fast greedy heuristic sorting eligible patients by immediate marginal harm $\Delta H$. Explicitly labelled as a proxy baseline.

---

## 5. Experimental Design & Sweeps
- **Facility Configuration:** 2 Linear Accelerators (LINAC-01, LINAC-02), each with daily capacity of 16 treatment fractions (total 32 slots/day), reflecting standard Nigerian tertiary radiotherapy center configurations (E6).
- **Patient Arrival Process:** Poisson arrival process with arrival rate sweep:
  $$\lambda \in [2.0, 2.5, 3.0, 3.5] \text{ patients/day}$$
  With mean fractions per course $\bar{F} \approx 18$, $\lambda \ge 2.0$ represents nominal slot demand $\ge 36$ fractions/day on a 32-slot facility (utilization $\ge 100\%$), driving wait times to weeks.
- **Machine Failure & Repair Process:**
  - Mean Time Between Failures ($\text{MTBF}$) = 21 days per LINAC.
  - Mean Time To Repair ($\text{MTTR}$) sweep: $\text{MTTR} \in [3, 7, 14]$ days (lognormal/exponential distribution).
- **Simulation Horizon:** 60 calendar days per replication; decision step every 1 day; rolling horizon window = 14 days.
- **Replications:** $\ge 50$ Monte Carlo simulation replications per parameter cell with fixed random seeds for exact reproducibility.

---

## 6. Statistical Methods
- **Effect Estimation:** Paired difference per replication: $\Delta_{\text{harm}} = \text{Harm}_{\text{baseline}} - \text{Harm}_{\text{CPSAT}}$.
- **Uncertainty Quantification:** Single pre-registered method: paired bootstrap over replications with $B = 1,000$ resamples. 95% confidence intervals reported as percentile bootstrap bounds $[q_{0.025}, q_{0.975}]$.
- **One Method Invariant:** No mixing of permutation p-values with bootstrap CIs; test decisions are governed strictly by whether the paired bootstrap 95% CI excludes zero.
