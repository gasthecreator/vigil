## 2. Verbatim Excerpts & Formulations

### From Aarsand et al. 2021 (*Clin Chem Lab Med*, 60:238–248):
> **Abstract (Background & Results):**
> "The European Biological Variation Study (EuBIVAS) was established by the EFLM Biological Variation Working Group to deliver BV data for clinically important measurands. In this study, EuBIVAS-based BV estimates are provided for cancer antigen (CA) 125, CA 15-3, CA 19-9, carcinoembryonic antigen, cytokeratin-19 fragment, alpha-fetoprotein and human epididymis protein 4."
> 
> "Marked individuality was found for all tumor markers."
> 
> "This study provides updated BV estimates for several tumor markers, and the findings indicate that marked individuality is characteristic. The use of reference change values should be considered when monitoring treatment of patients by means of tumor markers."

*(Full-Text Paper Table 2 — Individual BV Estimates):*
- **CA 15-3:** Within-subject variation $CV_i = 6.1\%$ (95% CI 5.6%–6.7%), between-subject variation $CV_g = 43.1\%$, Index of Individuality ($\text{II}$) = 0.14.
- **CEA:** Within-subject variation $CV_i = 12.7\%$ (95% CI 11.8%–13.8%), between-subject variation $CV_g = 55.4\%$, Index of Individuality ($\text{II}$) = 0.23.

### From Fraser 2001 (*Biological Variation: From Principles to Practice*):
> **Reference Change Value (RCV) Formula:**
> $$\text{RCV} = 2^{1/2} \times Z \times \sqrt{CV_a^2 + CV_i^2} = 1.414 \times Z \times \sqrt{CV_a^2 + CV_i^2}$$
> Where:
> - $CV_a$ is analytical imprecision of the laboratory assay.
> - $CV_i$ is within-subject biological variation.
> - $Z$ is the standard normal deviate ($Z = 1.96$ for 95% bidirectional confidence, $Z = 1.645$ for 95% unidirectional rise).
> 
> **Index of Individuality (II):**
> $$\text{II} = \frac{CV_i}{CV_g}$$
> When $\text{II} < 0.6$, conventional static population reference intervals lack sensitivity for individual monitoring, because a patient's value can change significantly above their baseline while remaining within the broad population reference limits.
