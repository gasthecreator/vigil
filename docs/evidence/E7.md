## 2. Verbatim Excerpts & Exact Reported Numbers

### From Jedy-Agba et al. 2016 (*Lancet Glob Health*, 4:e923–e935):
> **Abstract (Findings):**
> "83 studies were included, which consisted of 26 788 women from 17 sub-Saharan African countries."
> 
> "There was wide between-study heterogeneity in the percentage of late-stage disease at diagnosis (median 74·7%, range 30·3-100%, I2=93·3%, p<0·0001)."

### From Jedy-Agba et al. 2016 Table 2 & Results Excerpt:
> **Saved Excerpt:** [`docs/evidence/excerpts/E7_jedy_agba_2016_stage_africa.md`](excerpts/E7_jedy_agba_2016_stage_africa.md)

*(Derived / Full-Text Comparison — Jedy-Agba et al. Table 2 / Discussion):* In black women in sub-Saharan Africa, stage III/IV disease accounted for 74.7% of diagnoses, compared with 29.7% in white women and 37.8% in black women in the US SEER registry.

### From Huo et al. 2009 (*J Clin Oncol*, 27:4515–4521):
> **Abstract (Patients & Methods):**
> "The study comprised 507 patients diagnosed with breast cancer between 1996 and 2007 at six geographic regions in Nigeria and Senegal. Formalin-fixed and paraffin-embedded sections were constructed into tissue microarrays and immunostained with 15 antibodies."
> 
> **Abstract (Results):**
> "The mean (+/- standard deviation) age of 378 patients in the first cohort was 44.8 +/- 11.8 years, with the majority of women presenting with large (4.4 +/- 2.0 cm) high-grade tumors (83%) in advanced stages (72% node positive)."
> 
> "The proportions of estrogen receptor (ER) -positive, progesterone receptor-positive, and human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2) -positive tumors were 24%, 20%, and 17%, respectively."
> 
> "Triple negativity for these markers was predominant, including basal-like (27%) and unclassified subtype (28%). Other subtypes were luminal A (27%), luminal B (2%), and HER2 positive/ER negative (15%). The findings were replicated in the second cohort of 129 patients."

*(Derived Calculation):* Total triple-negative phenotype: 27% (basal-like) + 28% (unclassified) = 55% in Cohort 1.
