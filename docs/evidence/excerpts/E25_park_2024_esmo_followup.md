## Verbatim Excerpt: Section 8, Follow-up, long-term implications and survivorship

> "8a. Regular follow-up visits are recommended every 3 months in the first 3 years post-treatment (every 6 months for low-risk EBC), every 6 months from years 4-5 and annually thereafter. The interval of visits can be adapted to the risk of relapse and patient needs [V, A]"

> "8d. In asymptomatic patients, laboratory tests (e.g. blood counts, routine chemistry, tumour marker assessment) or other non-breast imaging for detection of relapse are not recommended [I, D] but may be considered on an individual basis [V, C]"

---

## What the K6 study takes from it (not quoted)
- The fixed 3-month and 6-month schedules of `experiments/EXP-K6-001` are the schedules named in 8a. They are the baselines the program (`docs/ML_PROGRAM.md`, study S6) asks K6 to be compared with.
- 8a says the interval "can be adapted to the risk of relapse", which is the idea of a risk-triggered schedule.

## Limits (not quoted)
- **8d is a counter-indication to the premise of marker-based surveillance.** Current guidance in the settings the guideline addresses does not recommend routine blood tests or tumour markers in asymptomatic patients. The K6 study evaluates marker-based schedules *inside a simulator*; nothing in it says that such surveillance helps real patients, and the guideline is evidence that benefit has not been shown for them. Any claim that follows from the study is limited to the simulator.
- The guideline is for Asian health systems (an adaptation of a European guideline). It is not a statement about Nigerian care pathways.
- The recommendations are consensus statements with the evidence levels shown inside the quotation; no number from the guideline's evidence tables is used.
