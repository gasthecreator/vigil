# Excerpt: Imaging Sensitivity for Hepatic Metastases (Kinkel et al. 2002)

**Source:** Kinkel K, Lu Y, Both M, Warren RS, Thoeni RF. "Detection of hepatic metastases from cancers of the gastrointestinal tract by using noninvasive imaging methods (US, CT, MR imaging, PET): a meta-analysis." *Radiology*. 2002;224(3):748-756.  
**DOI:** 10.1148/radiol.2243011362  
**PMID:** 12202709  
**License:** Copyright RSNA 2002. Only the PubMed abstract is quoted.  

---

## Verbatim Excerpt: Abstract, Results

> "Among 111 data sets, nine US (509 patients), 25 CT (1,747 patients), 11 MR imaging (401 patients), and nine PET (423 patients) data sets met the inclusion criteria. In studies with a specificity higher than 85%, the mean weighted sensitivity was 55% (95% CI: 41, 68) for US, 72% (95% CI: 63, 80) for CT, 76% (95% CI: 57, 91) for MR imaging, and 90% (95% CI: 80, 97) for FDG PET."

---

## Limits (not quoted)
- Populations are colorectal, gastric and esophageal cancers, not breast cancer.
- The sensitivities are reported within the stratum of data sets whose specificity is above 85 percent. No pooled specificity is given in the abstract; M4 uses the interval 0.85 to 1.0 for specificity and states that 1.0 is a logical bound.
- Published 2002; imaging technology has changed.
- The full text was not read.
