# EXP-K6-001: active sensing (K6), a risk-adaptive test schedule against fixed schedules at equal cost

**Status:** PRE-REGISTERED. This file is committed **alone**, before any K6 code, any surveillance-simulation code and any run. Everything below is fixed at that commit; any later change is an *amendment* appended at the end with its reason and the `git diff`.

**Authority:** `docs/ML_PROGRAM.md` §3.6 (K6) and study S6 (question: "Does active sensing beat fixed schedules at equal test budget (simulation)?"; kill rule: "If not → ship risk-triggered schedule suggestions only"), `docs/MASTER_PLAN.md` §13 (elimination protocol). Data: **synthetic only**. Nothing here is evidence about real patients.

**What I looked at before writing this file:** the simulator source (`vigil_sim/generators.py`), the M4 ladder contract and facility templates, the old K6 stub and the retracted S6 pre-registration, the speed of generating a dense world (17 ms per patient), and the follow-up recommendations in E25. I did **not** run any policy, any hazard model or any comparison between schedules.

## 1. Why this replaces the earlier S6
The earlier S6 (`reports/ml/RETRACTED/S6_ACTIVE_SENSING/`) is retracted. `vigil_ml/active_sensing_k6.py::recommend_next_action` is a hand-written `if` ladder on a risk number (thresholds 0.10, 0.15, 0.45 and fixed intervals 14, 45 and 180 days, with "expected information gain" values typed as constants 0.12, 0.48, 0.85 and 0.25), and the old study returned literal dictionaries. No policy was ever simulated against a patient.

Two further points that shape this design:
1. **E25 is a counter-indication.** The ESMO follow-up recommendations say that laboratory tests and tumour markers are *not* recommended in asymptomatic patients [I, D] (they "may be considered on an individual basis"), and that the visit interval "can be adapted to the risk of relapse". This study evaluates marker-based schedules **inside a simulator whose markers carry signal by construction**; it cannot show that such surveillance helps a real patient, and the guideline is evidence that benefit has not been shown for them.
2. **M4 refuses an exchange rate.** `docs/ml/M4-CONTRACT.md` does not turn price, delay and information into one score, because no repository evidence fixes the rate. K6 has to put cost and delay on one scale to choose a schedule. This study therefore never picks a rate: the cost-of-delay weight `w` is **swept over a fixed grid**, the result is a **frontier** (delay against cost), and the comparison with fixed schedules is made **at equal cost**, with no weight chosen. Prices are not real: costs are in **units** (section 3), an assumption, and every cost statement carries that label.

## 2. Question
At the same mean cost per patient, does a schedule that adapts the next visit time and test panel to a calibrated individual hazard (K6) detect recurrence earlier than (a) fixed schedules, including the 3-month and 6-month schedules of E25, (b) a stage-based clinician heuristic, and (c) a simple risk-triggered schedule that uses the same hazard (the S6 fallback)? And how much cost does K6 save at the delay that the 3-month fixed schedule achieves?

## 3. Simulation (the truth is known)
**Worlds are dense.** For every patient the simulator is called once on a grid of **weekly days**, `day_k = 182 + 7k`, `k = 0..234` (last day 1820): `g.sample_nigerian_baseline(i)`, then `g.simulate_longitudinal_trajectory(pt, grid)` and `g.generate_symptoms(pt, d, latent)` for each grid day. A policy chooses which grid days to *observe*. The simulator's observation noise and missingness at a grid day are drawn once, so a policy that visits that day sees the same measurement as any other policy that visits it (a deliberate pairing of policies). All three generators are functions of the day only (checked by reading the code), so the dense call is equivalent to calling them at the chosen days.
- **Surveillance starts at day 182** (after the generator's adjuvant-chemotherapy window, which ends at day 180). The visit at `k = 0` is a baseline visit with the full panel P3 for every patient and every policy.
- **Eligibility (all arms alike):** a patient is eligible if there is no competing event (death or loss to follow-up) before day 182 and, if the patient has a true recurrence, `true_recurrence_day ≥ 210`. The numbers excluded are reported. A competing event ends follow-up (no visits and no symptomatic presentation after it).
- **Generators and cohorts.** Training world: G1 (`GeneratorType.G1_SMOOTH`), 1800 generated patients. Test worlds: G1 (primary, 1200 generated), G2 and G3 (1200 each; descriptive, with everything trained on G1 only). The `latent_*` columns, `true_recurrence_*` and burdens are read only to build **labels and endpoints**, never as policy inputs (section 8).
- **Detectable day** `T_det`: the first grid day on which the sum of the five latent burden compartments is at least **12.0**, the radiological-resolution constant of the simulator's own verification mechanism (`evaluate_verification_mechanism`). If never, `T_det = ∞`. It is the earliest day on which imaging could confirm disease.
- **Symptomatic presentation** (scenario A, primary): on the first grid day on which the maximum symptom grade is at least 3, the patient presents unscheduled and is treated as detected that day. This is an **assumption** (no presentation delay, imaging taken to be positive, same for every policy); the runner reports the share of presentations at which the burden is below 12.0, which should be zero. **Scenario B (secondary)** removes it: detection is by screening only (the visit rule below still refers a patient with a symptom grade of 2 or more).
- **Scheduling grid.** Intervals are multiples of 7 days: the **action grid** is `Δ ∈ {14, 28, 42, 56, 84, 112, 168, 252, 364}` days for K6 and the risk-triggered family; the **fixed grid** is `Δ ∈ {28, 42, 56, 91, 119, 182, 273, 364}`. Visits happen exactly when scheduled (perfect attendance, an assumption; real adherence falls with visit frequency and with out-of-pocket cost, and this favours short intervals).

**Panels** (the visit always includes the symptom check, free):
| Panel | Analytes | Cost units |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | ALP, GGT, AST, ALT | 1 |
| P2 | P1 + CA 15-3 | 2 |
| P3 | P2 + CEA | 3 |

Costs: **visit 1 unit**, panel as above, **imaging referral 4 units** (one per alert, plus its visit). These are **assumptions in "price-band units"**: M4's registry holds ordinal price bands 1 to 4 and no money, and has no row for tumour markers; the mapping panel → band (1, 2, 3) and the visit and imaging units are mine. The simulator's own missingness applies (G1: CA 15-3 and CEA skipped with probability `0.12 × out_of_pocket_delay_factor`, every other analyte 0.03; G2: 0.05 for every analyte; G3: 0.06): a skipped analyte is billed but returns nothing. Sensitivity to other cost units (rescoring the stored runs, K6 not re-planned): visit ∈ {0.5, 3} and geometric panel units {1, 2, 4}.

**Detection mechanism (common to every policy; K6 only schedules).**
1. *Alert rule A.* At a visit after the baseline, for every analyte `a` in the panel that was measured, `z_a = log(value) − mean(log of all earlier measured values of a)` (needs at least one earlier value). The visit **alerts** if `z_a ≥ θ_a` for any such analyte **or** the maximum symptom grade is at least 2.
2. `θ_a` is the **0.995 quantile** of `z_a` over all post-baseline visits of **non-recurrent** patients in the training world on a fixed 91-day schedule with the full panel P3. (So each analyte raises about 0.5 percent false alerts per measurement at the training generator; there is no clinical meaning in the number.)
3. *Imaging.* An alert triggers one imaging referral. Imaging is **positive iff the burden at that day is at least 12.0**; then the patient is detected (screen-detected) and surveillance ends. A negative referral (an overdetection referral: markers moved, no disease large enough to see) leaves the patient under surveillance and costs 4 units.
4. `t_det` (detection day) is the minimum of the screen-detection day and, in scenario A, the symptomatic presentation day.

## 4. Policies
**Fixed (the strongest-baseline family).** `F(Δ, π)` for Δ on the fixed grid and π ∈ {P1, P2, P3}: 24 arms; the named baselines are `F(91, P2)` (3 months) and `F(182, P2)` (6 months). **Clinician heuristic:** stage I every 364 days, stage II every 182, stage III and IV every 91, panel P2 (illustrative; not from a guideline; E25 8a is the guideline reference).
**Risk-triggered (the S6 fallback, same hazard as K6).** Interval `Δ_short` if the calibrated hazard `F_182` (below) is at least `θ`, else `Δ_long`; panel P2. Grid `Δ_long ∈ {119, 182, 273, 364}`, `Δ_short ∈ {28, 56, 91}`, `θ ∈ {0.05, 0.10, 0.20, 0.40}`: 48 arms.
**K6 (the policy under test).** At every visit, after the patient's data are in, a **calibrated multi-horizon hazard model** returns `G(u)`, the probability that the patient's detectable day falls on or before `u` days from now, given that the patient is still under surveillance (undetected), for `u ∈ {0, 28, 56, 91, 182, 364}`. `G` is the piecewise-linear interpolation through the knots (flat after 364) after enforcing monotonicity (running maximum across horizons). For every available action `(Δ, π)` K6 computes the **expected cost per day**

```
A(Δ)    = integral from 0 to Δ of G(u) du                       (expected days of disease present and undetected before the next test)
s_π, f_π = detection probability of rule A given disease detectable, and false-alert probability, with panel π (section 5)
C(Δ,π)  = c_visit + c_π + [ (1 - G(Δ)) f_π + G(Δ) s_π ] c_img
          + w * ( A(Δ) + G(Δ) (1 - s_π) / s_π * Δ )              (a missed disease persists for a geometric number of further intervals)
K6 picks  argmin over (Δ, π) of C(Δ, π) / Δ.
```
`w` (units of cost per day of undetected detectable disease) takes the grid **`{0.1, 0.2, 0.4, 0.8, 1.6, 3.2, 6.4, 12.8, 25.6}`**; each value is one K6 arm, and all nine are reported. This is the "expected net benefit per unit cost" formulation of `docs/ML_PROGRAM.md` §3.6. **Not built, and not evaluated here:** posterior expected information gain by nested Monte Carlo on the K1 posterior, offline reinforcement learning, and conservative off-policy evaluation. Reason, plainly: a K1 posterior per decision for every patient and every candidate action was not attempted, and the plan's v1.3 re-ordering note (`docs/MASTER_PLAN.md`) records that K1 did not beat K3 and B4 on the simulator. The fixed one-step cost model above is a simplification (it ignores patients who become detectable after the next test, and assumes the same action repeats after a miss).
**K6 variants (descriptive ablations, same nine `w`):** P1 only, P2 only, P3 only (the interval is still chosen), and **prior-only** (the hazard model sees only static covariates and the time since surveillance began, not the laboratory history; panels free).

## 5. The hazard model, its training and the panel table (all inside the replication, on the training world only)
- **Features (observed data only, causal):** static: stage ordinal 1 to 4, TNBC, HER2-positive, ER, PR, age; time: days since day 182, number of visits, last interval; for each of the six analytes: days since last measured, `z` at the last measurement, running maximum of `z`, log ratio of the last value to the first, change in log value per 90 days between the last two measurements; global: maximum symptom grade now, number of negative imaging referrals, days since the last alert. Missing quantities are `NaN` (XGBoost's native handling).
- **Training rows:** every training patient is run through **3 random schedules** (a patient-level base interval drawn uniformly from `{14, 28, 56, 91, 182, 364}`; at each visit the next interval is the base with probability 0.7, else drawn uniformly from the action grid; the panel at each visit drawn uniformly from P1, P2, P3). A row is created at every visit while the patient is undetected and not yet presenting. Label for horizon `u`: `T_det ≤ day_now + u`.
- **Model:** one `xgboost.XGBClassifier` per horizon (depth 3, 120 trees, learning rate 0.1, subsample 0.8, column subsample 0.8, `min_child_weight` 20, `reg_lambda` 5, `n_jobs` 1, fixed seed), fitted on a random 75 percent of the training patients; **isotonic regression** per horizon fitted on the other 25 percent (grouped by patient) for calibration; running maximum across horizons. No hyper-parameter is tuned.
- **Panel table:** `s_π` = share of training-world visits with burden at least 12 at which rule A, restricted to panel π's analytes plus the symptom rule, alerts; `f_π` = the same among visits with burden below 12. Both are computed counterfactually on every training visit for all three panels (the same history, a different set of analytes).
- **Persisted without pickle:** the models are saved as XGBoost JSON text and the isotonic knots as arrays, in a dictionary that round-trips to an identical `G` (section 8).

## 6. Endpoints and decision rules
**Per policy arm, per replication (the script writes all of them):**
- **Cost** = mean over eligible patients of visit + panel + imaging units until detection or censoring, within the surveillance window (days 182 to 1820). In scenario A visits on or after the symptomatic presentation day are not counted.
- **Delay (primary).** Delay cohort: recurrent patients with `true_recurrence_day ∈ [210, 1455]` and no competing event before `true_recurrence_day + 365`. For each, `delay = min(t_det − true_recurrence_day, 365)` days, with **365 if not detected** by the end of follow-up. Negative is earlier than clinical recurrence. The reported number is the **mean delay over the delay cohort** (days, lower is better). Companion: the share missed (delay 365), the share detected at least 28 days before clinical recurrence, the share screen-detected before symptomatic presentation.
- **Overdetection:** imaging referrals with burden below 12, per 100 patient-years of follow-up.
- **Visits per patient.** For K6 arms: the share of visits by panel.
**Frontier and comparison at equal cost.** For a family of arms, pool the per-replication means; the **frontier** is the lower-left Pareto set in (cost, delay) (an arm is on it if no cheaper-or-equal arm has a lower-or-equal delay), joined by straight lines; outside the cost range of a family's frontier its value is *not defined*. Budgets `B13` and `B26` are the pooled frozen costs of `F(91, P2)` and `F(182, P2)` (constants in the bootstrap). `D13` is the pooled frozen delay of `F(91, P2)`.
**Multiplicity.** Primary family `{H1, H2, H3}`; paired bootstrap **over replications** (B = 2000, RNG seed 20261215; each bootstrap draw resamples the replications with replacement, recomputes every arm's mean, every frontier and the statistic), percentile intervals at level `1 − 0.05/3` (Bonferroni). Everything else is descriptive with 95 percent intervals and supports no claim.
- **H1 (K6 beats fixed schedules at equal cost).** `S1` = mean over the budgets `{B13, B26}` of `delay_K6frontier(B) − delay_fixedfrontier(B)` (the fixed frontier is the frontier of all 24 fixed arms). **Met** if the upper bound of the interval for `S1` is below 0.
- **H2 (K6 beats the risk-triggered schedule at equal cost).** `S2` = the same with the 48-arm risk-triggered frontier. **Met** if the upper bound of the interval for `S2` is below 0.
- **H3 (cost saving at the delay of the 3-month schedule).** `S3 = 1 − cost_K6frontier(D13) / cost_fixedfrontier(D13)` (the frontiers read at delay `D13`). **Met** if the lower bound of the interval for `S3` is above 0.25. (The 25 percent is the earlier S6's number, kept as a stated target; it is not evidence-based.)
- A statistic that cannot be computed because a frontier does not cover the budget or `D13` is reported as "not covered" and the hypothesis is **not met**.
**Kill and fallback (from `docs/ML_PROGRAM.md` study S6).** If H1 fails, K6 is **not claimed** to beat fixed schedules and nothing about adaptive scheduling is recommended on this evidence. If H1 holds and H2 fails, the recommendation is **risk-triggered schedule suggestions only**. If H1 and H2 both hold, K6 is still only ever a *suggestion with its reasoning shown*, and only a simulation claim; no clinical benefit is claimed (`ML_PROGRAM` §3.6). A pre-registered failure is an acceptable outcome (§13).
**Leakage canary (can void the study, one-sided).** In every replication, 5 K6 fits are made with the hazard model trained on labels **shuffled independently over rows** (a different shuffle per horizon, fixed seeds), true panel table and true features. Canary statistic `Cn` = the mean over replications and shuffles of the K6 frontier minus the fixed frontier, averaged over `{B13, B26}` (days; positive is worse). **The study is VOID if the upper end of the 95 percent bootstrap-over-replications interval of `Cn` is below 0**, i.e. a K6 without information about the patient beats fixed schedules (that would mean information is reaching the policy some other way). The rule is one-sided on purpose: a K6 without information can be no better than a fixed schedule except by noise, and may be worse. If it is void, every result is declared void in `CONCLUSION.md`.
**Not hypotheses, always reported:** the frontiers of all families and variants; K6 minus the fixed frontier at `B13`, `B26` and at the budget geometric-halfway between them; scenario B; the clinician heuristic as a point against the fixed frontier; cross-generator (G2 and G3) results with the same readings; hazard calibration (reliability table and ECE for `G(182)` on the test world); the panel mix and interval mix chosen by K6 by `w`; **policy stability** (below); the cost-unit sensitivity.
**Policy stability (descriptive).** For every K6 decision of the arm `w = 1.6`, perturb every knot of `G` by an independent uniform draw in `[-0.02, +0.02]`, re-enforce monotonicity and decide again; report the share of decisions whose interval moves by more than one step of the action grid and the share whose panel changes.

## 7. Seeds, development and frozen evaluation
- **Development replications** `r ∈ {0, 1, 2}`, base seed `s_r = 900 + 100 r`. **Frozen replications** `r ∈ {0..19}`, `s_r = 20261215 + 100 r`. Per replication: training world seed `s_r`, test worlds `s_r + 1` (G1), `+2` (G2), `+3` (G3); training-schedule randomisation `+4`; model seed `+5`; canary shuffles `+6 + j` (`j = 0..4`); stability perturbation `+11`.
- All debugging, the check that the frontiers cover the budgets, and the calibration look use only the development replications. The development file is committed.
- **The frozen run is executed once.** If code changes afterwards, the old result stays committed and an amendment and a new run are added. The `w` grid, the action grid, the model settings, the alert rule, the costs and the hypotheses are fixed here. If the development replications show that the K6 frontier does not reach `B13` and `B26`, the only permitted change is to extend the `w` grid by doubling or halving at its ends, by an amendment before the frozen run.
- Every number in the report is written by the script (`experiments/EXP-K6-001/run.py`); none is typed.

## 8. Invariants tested before the frozen run (`packages/vigil-ml/tests/test_k6.py`, `packages/vigil-sim/tests/test_surveillance.py`)
1. **Monotone in hazard:** if `G` is raised pointwise, the interval K6 chooses (for fixed `w` and panel set) never becomes longer.
2. **Limits in `w`:** as `w → 0` K6 chooses the longest interval and the cheapest panel when `G ≡ 0`; as `w → ∞` with a high `G` it chooses the shortest interval.
3. **Availability (M4 integration):** a panel marked unavailable is never recommended; with no panel available the planner returns "no option" and never a schedule; cost units are inputs, so changing the panel prices changes the choice in the expected direction.
4. **Hazard output:** every `G(u)` lies in `[0, 1]` and is non-decreasing in `u`.
5. **Causality:** changing any observation at or after the next visit leaves the features of the current visit unchanged; features do not change when the evaluation-only arrays (`latent` burdens, true recurrence day, `T_det`) are replaced by garbage.
6. **The policies never read evaluation columns:** an AST test of `k6.py` and of the policy path in the surveillance engine (no `latent_`, `true_recurrence`, `burden`, `T_det`), and a test that the decisions of every policy are identical when those arrays are scrambled.
7. **Determinism and pairing:** the same seed gives identical worlds and identical policy outcomes; two policies that visit the same day see the identical measurement.
8. **Export parity:** the saved hazard model reproduces `G` exactly after a round trip, and its serialised form contains no pickle.
9. **Planted signal:** in a toy world where the markers jump on the detectable day, K6 detects earlier than the fixed schedule of the same cost, and the same K6 with shuffled-label hazard (mean of 5 shuffles) does not.
10. **Planner stability:** with random `G` and perturbations of ±0.005, the interval moves by more than one grid step in under 10 percent of cases.

## 9. What will be reported regardless of outcome
All frontiers and ablations, strongest baseline first; the canary; the development file; H1 to H3 as met or not met with numbers from `results.json`; scenario B; cross-generator results; run time. If a kill rule fires, `experiments/EXP-K6-001/CONCLUSION.md` is the elimination record (what was tried, the §13.3 checklist, what ships instead, what would change the decision). The word "infeasible" is not used unless §13.2's three serious attempts are met.

## 10. Not in scope (stated as limits, not hidden)
Real patients, real prices, real attendance and out-of-pocket delay; EIG by nested Monte Carlo on K1; reinforcement learning; per-site or per-lab effects; K5 harmonisation; integration of K6 into the API or the web app (K6 ships, if at all, as a suggestion with its reasoning, and nothing in this study wires it in); ctDNA and imaging-based surveillance panels; treatment changes after detection; the guideline recommendation (E25 8d) against routine markers in asymptomatic patients.

## Amendments
### Amendment 1 (before any K6 code and before any run): the planner's objective was wrong; replaced by a finite-horizon closed form
**What was wrong.** Section 4 minimises the cost *per day* of one cycle, `C(Δ,π)/Δ`, with `A(Δ)` as the days of undetected disease in the cycle. I checked it by hand while starting to write the planner. For a patient who is already probably diseased (`G` constant at `g`), `A(Δ)/Δ = g` and the delay term of `C/Δ` is the constant `w g / s` for every `Δ`, so the visit cost `c/Δ` alone decides and K6 would choose the **longest** interval for a patient it believes is already ill. Dividing by `Δ` charges a short interval for its tests every cycle but does not credit it for finding present disease sooner. This is a defect of my formula, found before any code or run existed.
**Replacement (fixed here).** Plan over a fixed horizon `H = 364` days. For an action `(Δ, π)` with the same panel and interval repeated:
```
J(Δ,π) = (H/Δ) * [ c_visit + c_π + f_π * c_img * (1 - Ḡ) ]
       + w * Δ * [ G(0)/s_π + (G(H) - G(0)) * ( 1/2 + (1 - s_π)/s_π ) ]
K6 picks argmin over (Δ, π) of J(Δ, π)
```
where `Ḡ = (1/H) ∫_0^H G(u) du` (exact for the piecewise-linear `G`), `s_π` is floored at 0.02, and the cost of the imaging that confirms a true detection is omitted because it is the same for every action. Reading: a patient already detectable (probability `G(0)`) waits on average `Δ/s_π` days for a detection (first test after `Δ`, then a geometric number of further intervals); a patient who becomes detectable during the horizon (probability `G(H) − G(0)`) waits on average `Δ/2` to the next test (phase averaged) plus the geometric term. It ignores truncation at `H`, onsets after `H`, and the phase of the schedule. `w` keeps the same meaning (cost units per day of undetected detectable disease) and the same grid. Only `G(0)`, `G(H)` and `Ḡ` enter, so the intermediate knots matter through `Ḡ`.
**Consequences for section 8.** `J = a/Δ + bΔ` for a fixed panel, with `a` decreasing and `b` increasing in `G`, so for a **single available panel** the chosen interval is non-increasing as `G` is raised pointwise (provable; `a/(Δ1 Δ2) < b` decides each pair). Invariant 1 is therefore stated for each single panel; with several panels the choice of panel may change as `G` rises and the **joint** choice of interval is not claimed monotone (it is reported). Invariant 2 stands as written (`G ≡ 0` gives `b = 0`, so the longest interval and the panel with the smallest `c_visit + c_π + f_π c_img`; a high `G(H)` with `w → ∞` gives the shortest interval). Nothing else changes: grids, hazard model, panel table, alert rule, costs, hypotheses, kill rules, seeds.

### Amendment 2 (before any run; decided while writing the runner): bootstrap draws that cannot be computed, and the canary's per-replication frontier
**Bootstrap draws.** Section 6 says a statistic that cannot be computed is "not covered" and the hypothesis is not met; it does not say what a bootstrap draw does when a resampled frontier does not cover a budget. Rule fixed here: draws in which any frontier needed for the statistic does not cover the budget (or the delay `D13`) are **dropped**, the interval is computed from the remaining draws, and the share dropped is reported. A hypothesis is **not met** if the point estimate is not covered or if more than 5 percent of the draws are dropped.
**Canary.** `Cn` is computed per replication and shuffle from that replication's own arms: the K6 frontier of the shuffled model (nine `w` values, that replication only) minus the fixed frontier of the same replication, read at the two budgets `B13` and `B26` (constants from the pooled frozen sample). A (replication, shuffle, budget) triple in which a frontier does not cover the budget is skipped and counted. Frontiers built from a single replication take the best of the arms for each cost, which lowers both sides alike; this is a noisier but unbiased-in-direction version of the pooled comparison.
**Cost-unit sensitivity.** The alternative cost units of section 3 are applied to the stored visits, panel shares and alerts per patient of every arm (K6 is not re-planned); the point estimate of `S1` is reported under each, without intervals.
Nothing else changes.
